Four layers, one recommendation

Each advisory is built from real data, not a generic template. This is the part that took the longest to get right — and it's the part that matters most.

1

Satellite crop health

NDVI and vegetation indices show how a plot is actually performing — not what a farmer assumes from the ground.

2

Soil health records

Nutrient levels, moisture, and degradation trends, pulled from soil health card data where available.

3

Weather forecasting

Short and medium-range forecasts, so a recommendation accounts for what's coming, not just what's happened.

4

Regenerative recommendation engine

The three layers combine into one plain-language advisory — rotation, treatment, or timing — plus an optional crop disease check from an uploaded photo.

Built for two readers

The same underlying data serves a farmer deciding what to do this week, and a policymaker deciding where to direct resources this season.

Not another disease-ID app

Photo-based crop disease detection already exists, and it's not the point. Here's the actual difference.

What a disease-ID app does

Takes a photo, names a disease. Stops there.

No soil, weather, or regional context.

Nothing for a district office to act on.

What AgriWis does

Combines satellite, soil, and weather into one recommendation — disease check is one feature inside it, not the whole product.

Produces district-level trends a policymaker can actually use.

Shares its data schema, so it works as shared infrastructure, not a single app.

A field's story, told in one recommendation.

AgriWis is built to be small enough for one farmer to use this week, and open enough for a state to build on next year.
